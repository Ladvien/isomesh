# The novelty table — opportunities, their literature, and what they look like in the game

**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Source:** eight parallel corpus sweeps (primal extraction, dual extraction, adaptive/LOD/seams, vertex placement and error metrics, GPU and parallel, incremental and dynamic, learned and frontier, adjacent-math transfer).
**Tier:** every row is **R** — reported from literature, read by a sweep agent, not measured here. Where a row touches an existing **M** row in `FINDINGS.md`, the M row wins. Nothing below is a claim about this crate's behaviour.

**Excluded by design** — already banked, do not re-propose: Sabine reverberation on the breach frame, modal analysis of dug structures, masonry statics by alternating projection, the heat-method / Closest Point Method routing decision, medial axis and weak-feature-size certificates, contour-tree maintenance, persistence-thresholded ambiguity resolution, union-find connectivity over the air sublevel set.

## Rejected, with the reason

This section carries as much weight as the table above. Each of these looks transferable and is not.

- **Persistent-thread GPU kernels** (Aila & Laine; Gupta/Stuart/Owens). Persistent threads pay off when work units vary in traversal *depth*. An 8-corner case table is bounded, uniform work. Gupta et al.'s own caveat — PT "can also result in performance loss in many cases" — applies squarely. Wrong workload shape.
- **Task-donation queues for GPU load balancing** (Tzeng/Patney/Owens). The mechanism is real and the win is large, but queue order is nondeterministic *by construction*. Recovering byte-identical output needs a deterministic sort afterwards, which is not free and is not counted in the paper's numbers. Determinism is a hard constraint here, not a preference.
- **Span-space and interval-tree acceleration** (Livnat et al.; Cignoni et al.). Built for varying the isovalue on a *static* field. Here the field changes and the isovalue does not — the index would need rebuilding on every edit, which is precisely the cost it exists to avoid. Rejected direction, worth recording as such.
- **Progressive meshes as the LOD unit** (Hoppe). Construction is a one-time global optimisation over a static mesh. Mismatched to re-meshing thousands of destructible chunks per edit.
- **Convex Primitive Decomposition as a cutting substrate.** Already falsified at ✗21 — it covers the *surface*, its primitives *overlap by design*, and enclosure is the wrong guarantee for a substrate. Recorded here so it is not re-proposed a third time.
- **Learned per-cell topology** (Neural MC / Neural DC). The one genuinely network-bound mechanism in the learned literature. Strip the network and the nearest analytic substitute is Hermite normal clustering, which classical dual contouring already does. Nothing unique remains to extract.
- **CVT / Lloyd relaxation for placement.** Needs an iteration loop to converge. Amortised cost is fine offline and wrong for a 16.67 ms budget with thousands of chunks.
- **Restricted power diagrams with a global weight solve** (Lévy; Wang et al.). The weight solve is genuinely analytic — no network anywhere in the lineage, which makes it the most tempting row in the whole sweep. It is rejected for the hot path only because the Newton solve wants a *global* relaxation, and this crate meshes chunks independently. **Keep it live for the CAD consumer**, which is offline and where it may be the strongest option available.
- **Material point method, peridynamics, lattice Boltzmann.** All require owning a stateful timestep loop and material history, which breaks the "field in, triangles out" contract outright.
- **Wavelet and compressed-sensing volume compression.** Solves a storage problem the *consumer* owns, not one this crate has, and would cost a dependency for no meshing benefit.
- **Hierarchical matrices, randomised sketching, Krylov recycling.** The solve here is a dense 3×3 per vertex. These amortise cost over large sparse systems that do not exist in this architecture. Wrong cost class.

## Three notes on method

**The sweeps disagreed with each other, and the disagreements were the useful part.** Three of eight independently reported *Dual Contouring of Hermite Data* absent, citing DOI `10.1145/566654.566586`. That DOI does not resolve; the paper is present at `10.1145/566570.566586`. Any ticket carrying the first DOI has a bad citation.

**Every ABSENT claim in this session that was checked turned out to be wrong.** Three for three — Lorensen & Cline, van Gelder & Wilhelms, Kobbelt. The cause is the corpus metadata defect documented in the companion audit: 37.3% of search hits return `title: null`, so a paper can rank first and still be invisible. **No row above should be started on the belief that a paper is missing without checking `paper_download` first** — it answers "File already exists" in under a second.

**Read the paper before the row, not after.** Rows 3 and 15 rest on papers not yet in the corpus. Rows 1, 8 and 10 rest on papers that are in the corpus but have been read only in snippet form by a sweep agent. Rule 5 applies: a case table or a sign convention taken from a summary is exactly how a mesh comes out subtly non-manifold and looks fine.

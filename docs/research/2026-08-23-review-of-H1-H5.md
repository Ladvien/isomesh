## H5 — right target, and falsified as written by an entry already in the ledger

**M-177, verbatim:** *"A lattice rotation can negate a component, so a sum of positions or of
normal-outer-product entries is **not bit-exactly equivariant under the full octahedral group** by this
route."*

H5 predicts `mesh(g·f) = g·mesh(f)` bit-exactly for **all 48**. The ledger already predicts the
orientation-reversing half fails on the dual path, with a witness: `[1e-16, +1, −1]` sums to `1.11e-16`
and its negation to `0`. Registering H5 as written spends an experiment rediscovering M-177.

**The repair makes it the best of the five, because it becomes discriminating rather than confirmatory.**
Split the prediction by extractor family, because the families differ in exactly the way M-177 names:

> **C1.** The **primal** family — `marching_cubes`, `+decider`, `marching_tetrahedra`,
> `subgrid_marching_tetrahedra` — is bit-exactly equivariant under **all 48** elements. Their vertex is
> `a/(a−b)` on a single edge: two values, no accumulation, nothing to reorder.
>
> **C2.** The **dual** family — `surface_nets`, `dual_contouring`, `manifold_dual_contouring` — is
> bit-exactly equivariant under the **24 rotations** (M-24 measured 72/72 rotation×fixture cases
> bit-identical after the symmetric-determinant fix) and **fails on at least one reflection** on at
> least one field.
>
> **Falsified by** a primal failure, which is an axis-dependent bug and the thing H5 exists to catch;
> or by all 48 holding on the dual path, which would contradict M-177 and be the more interesting
> result. The **count and identity of failing reflections** is the new number either way.

**Fixture warning, and it is the third appearance of this trap.** M-178 records that `box_exact` and
`thin_plate` at 17³ and 33³ have exactly-representable crossing coordinates — *"the crossing coordinates
are exact, their sums are exact, and reordering cannot change a result that was never rounded."* Those
two fields at those two resolutions would pass the reflection clause **vacuously**. The run must include
`gyroid`, `fbm_terrain` or `noise_cavity`, and must assert that the fixture can fail.

Everything else about H5 is right: signed coordinate permutations are exact in floating point, no
tolerance is needed, and the motive is live — A-024 found an axis-dependent defect, and M-285's three
`const`-generic monomorphisations are exactly the code shape that grows another.

## H2 — the premise survives the check I expected it to fail, and the mechanism dies on the same code

**First, the good news, because it is the check that mattered.** I read `box_gradient`
(`fields/mod.rs:446`). It is **not** M-289's failure class. The exterior branch guards on
`max_q > R::ZERO`, with a comment stating exactly why: *"`max(q, 0)` is non-zero here, so the division
is safe — which is exactly why the branch is `> 0` and not `>= 0`."* The interior branch returns an
exact axis unit vector with no normalisation at all. **35.796° is not contaminated by the noise-gradient
bug**, and that was the live risk.

**Now the problem, from the same forty lines.** A Marching Cubes vertex lies **on** the surface, so
`box_gradient` takes the `else` branch and returns

```rust
let j = argmax(q);            // with a `>=` tie-break
g[j] = d[j].signum();         // exactly one of ±e_x, ±e_y, ±e_z
```

It returns **one axis, selected by a tie-break** — not a point in `conv{∇fᵢ : i active}`. So the
measured quantity is the angle between an area-weighted fan normal and a *single hull vertex*, not
between a normal and a Clarke hull. **The hull framing describes a different computation from the one
that produced the number.**

**And the arithmetic does not support C3.** Angular radius of the spherical hull of a 3-face box corner
`{e_x, e_y, e_z}` is `arccos(1/√3) = 54.7356°`; for a 2-face edge it is `45°`. Measured worst is
`35.796°`. The paste notices the 45 > 35.796 tension and offers two explanations. There is a third it
does not name, and it is the one the ledger has been burned by: **a bound being unattained is the normal
case.** C1 (the bound holds) and C3 (it predicts 35.796° to six figures) are logically independent — C3
needs a separate *attainment* argument, and M-283→M-289 is precisely the story of mistaking a bound for
a prediction and then reversing it.

**The cheap decisive test is not a derivation. It is a rotation.**

> Re-measure M-66's worst angle on `box_exact` **turned 17° and 37° about a body diagonal**, at 17³, 33³
> and 65³. If `35.796°` is corner geometry it survives; if it is grid alignment it moves.

Three things say it will move. M-178: `box_exact` at 17³/33³ has exact crossing arithmetic, so the worst
configuration is *exactly* self-similar under grid doubling — which is a sufficient explanation for six
identical figures with no theory involved. M-283: the wedge agreed with its prediction to **four decimal
places** aligned and gave **20.1°–128.0°** turned 17°. And Part 5's own rule: *"a fixture can exhibit the
property too perfectly, and exactness is the tell rather than the confirmation."* Six figures across
three resolutions on an axis-aligned box is that tell.

Run the control first. One afternoon, no acquisition, and it either earns the derivation or saves a week.

**Corpus:** Clarke / nonsmooth analysis is **absent** — best hit 0.566 and unrelated. The paste's "zero"
is correct.

## H3 — the strongest genuinely new one, with one clause that would fail for the instrument's reason

Keep it. Fix the set-equality clause.

**Interval methods fail conservatively.** A cell can be un-certified because the enclosure is loose, not
because the cell is ambiguous. Predicting **set equality** between "Krawczyk fails" and "the decider
fires" invites a falsification that indicts the interval extension rather than the world — the exact
failure mode ✗29, M-331 and M-326 each recorded once.

> **Predict containment:** every ambiguous cell is un-certified (`ambiguous ⊆ uncertified`), the excess
> `|uncertified \ ambiguous|` is under some registered fraction of active cells, and the excess is
> reported per field either way.

**And there is a reason this is unusually cheap here that belongs in the registration.** Krawczyk needs
an interval extension of the **derivative**, which is the expensive part in general. Every partial of a
trilinear is **multi-affine in the remaining two variables**, so its exact range over any sub-box is the
min and max of that sub-box's corner values — free, and *exact* rather than merely tight, since a
multi-affine function attains its extrema at box vertices. The hard ingredient is a `min` and a `max`.

**Corpus:** interval Newton / Krawczyk is **absent** — best hit 0.571, unrelated. Confirmed. But
Mourrain & Pavone `10.1016/j.jsc.2008.04.016` **is** in the corpus, and so is Reuter et al.'s
barycentric Bernstein polynomial-system solver `10.1007/s00371-007-0184-x` — neither cited anywhere in
the repo. Those are the subdivision half of the same machinery, already paid for.

## H1 — the hypothesis is fine; the mechanism story and the cost are both wrong, and P-56 already pays most of it

I ran this one to ground earlier today. Four corrections:

**Newton polytopes and dual subdivisions need affine pieces.** Sphere and capsule SDFs are not affine,
so "the dual subdivision of a Newton polytope" is not available for a `BrushStack`. The tape becomes
*literally* tropical only under the **power** substitution — `|x−c|² − r² = |x|² + min of affine` —
whose corner locus is a power diagram with the full toolkit. Measured cost of that substitution:
`argmin(power)` disagrees with `argmin(SDF)` on **16%** of samples. Accelerator, not substitute.

**For a pure-`Add` sphere tape the seam is the additively-weighted (Apollonius) Voronoi diagram** — by
definition, since `δ(x, Pᵢ) = ‖x − cᵢ‖ − wᵢ` *is* the sphere SDF. Forty years of literature, CGAL in 2D,
`10.1145/3730868` doing 100k sites in 8.1 s on a GPU in 3D. Tropical vocabulary adds no theorem here.

**The genuinely unnamed object is the mixed case**, and it is worth its own line: an `Add`/`Subtract`
bisector is `|x − cᵢ| + |x − cⱼ| = rᵢ + rⱼ`, a **prolate spheroid**, which no standard Voronoi diagram
produces and which has no name, no complexity bound better than Sharir's `O(n^{3+ε})`, and no
implementation. That is also the structural reason M-37 measured 11 distinct results from mixed ops.

**`O(brushes²)` is the wrong cost.** Deciding *whether* a cell can straddle is `O(brushes)` — the two
smallest values and their margin — which is what the P-56 certificate already computes inside the fold
that is already running. The pair only matters if you want to **name** it. So H1's marginal value over
P-56 is the pair's identity, not the prediction.
